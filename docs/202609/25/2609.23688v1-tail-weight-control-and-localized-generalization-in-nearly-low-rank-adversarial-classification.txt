Abstract. We study norm-constrained linear classification under Eu-clidean adversarial perturbations in a Gaussian model with a low-dimen-sional informative subspace and an independent noise tail. For bounded ramp loss, we prove that a principal-space witness with risk below one half forces every near-optimal predictor to have small tail weight. A path-specific density bound yields constants without requiring positive tail variance. Under isotropic principal covariance, we establish a unique population minimizer and joint local growth. Boundary normalization then removes the common attack penalty from centered margins, giving localized finite-sample guarantees governed by principal dimension and total tail energy. Globalized growth removes the entrance condition at weaker constants; a model-aware comparison retains local guarantees. Experiments with twenty paired repetitions show decreasing excess risk and tail use with sample size, and nearly unchanged behavior when tail dimension grows at fixed total energy. Pure-noise controls and optimizer diagnostics clarify the scope and limitations of these conclusions. 

Keywords: Adversarial classification, spectral tails, local complexity, excess risk, Gaussian mixtures 

## 1 Introduction 

Many observed coordinates may contain little label information, yet a learner trained in the full space can use them to fit a finite sample. Under adversarial perturbations, these coefficients also affect the worst-case margin. We study whether full-space training controls the weight assigned to an independent, uninformative tail, and how that structural control affects generalization. We study norm-constrained linear scores, a full-space Euclidean adversary and bounded ramp loss. Signal lies in a principal Gaussian block; the orthogonal tail is independent noise. The principal subspace defines an analytical oracle but is not supplied to full-space fitting. Deleting a noise coefficient need not improve a nonconvex bounded surrogate. A principal-space witness with risk below 1 /2 places near-optimal predictors in a favorable margin region, where tail deletion reduces the attack penalty without arXiv:2609.23688v1 [cs.LG] 20 Sep 2026 2 K. Wang, D. Wang, and W. Chen 

a harmful variance change. We prove joint growth under isotropy and obtain a fast-rate bound with explicit local constants after an entrance check. A second version globalizes the growth inequality and removes the entrance condition at the cost of weaker constants. 

Related Work. Adversarial Rademacher bounds for linear and nonlinear classes were developed in [2,12]; robust sample complexity can differ substantially from ordinary learning [10]. Dan et al. [6] establish sharp statistical guarantees for robust Gaussian classification. Javanmard and Soltanolkotabi [8] characterize classification accuracies in a proportional high-dimensional limit. Their estima-tor, loss or asymptotic regime differs from our finite-sample, fixed-radius ramp analysis. Chen et al. [5] study benign overfitting under adversarial training with an exponential objective. Tanner et al. [11] analyze feature blocks with heterogeneous usefulness and robustness. Their predictive but vulnerable blocks should not be identified with our independent zero-signal tail. Robust low-dimensional representations and latent models are studied in [1,7]; here the learner performs no preliminary projection. Our statistical argument follows local empirical-process methods [4]. Adversarial fast rates already appear in [9]; our contribution is to verify the centered-loss variance and geometry conditions for this nonsmooth ramp model. Finally, surrogate calibration requires separate assumptions [3]; the bounds below do not identify ramp and robust classification excess risks. Our contribution is explicit deletion growth, including zero tail variance, and its connection to finite-sample bounds through isotropic joint geometry and normalization. We claim neither the first adversarial fast rate nor a global nonconvex solver.

## 7 Discussion 

A tail-free population optimum does not prevent finite-sample tail use. The witness-based deletion inequality and isotropic geometry yield statistical bounds, while the experiments expose empirical fitting incentives relative to a simple initialization. The finite-sample constants remain conservative; general anisotropic growth and global optimization guarantees are unresolved. No same-task minimax lower bound is established: a principal-subspace oracle can remove the tail entirely. A vanishing attack, informative tail or non-Euclidean geometry requires separate arguments. Our bounds concern ramp excess risk. Although the isotropic model shares an optimal direction with robust classification, a quantitative excess-risk conversion has not been established here. 14 K.
